Vintage · 1898 · Shaken

Daiquiri

Ingredients

Servings 1
  • You have this: 60 mlWhite RumWhite Cuban rum
  • You have this: 20 mlLime Juice
  • You have this: 2 barspoonsSugarSuperfine sugar
Glass
Cocktail Glass
Garnish
No garnish

Method

  1. Add all ingredients to a cocktail shaker and stir to dissolve the sugar.
  2. Add ice and shake.
  3. Strain into a chilled cocktail glass.

How it tastes

Crisp, clean and perfectly simple. Light rum provides a gentle sugarcane sweetness and subtle vanilla, fresh lime brings vivid, mouth-watering sourness, and fine sugar rounds off the edges. Shaken hard and served very cold, it is bright and refreshing, with sweet and sour in close balance and no single flavour dominating. It is a benchmark for how good a three-ingredient sour can be.

The story

The Daiquiri is credited to Jennings Stockton Cox, an American mining engineer working near the small town of Daiquirí in southeastern Cuba around 1898. Difford's Guide reports that he experimented with the local Bacardi rum, mixing it with limes and sugar. A fellow engineer, Francesco Pagliuchi, later wrote a letter in 1948 backing Cox's claim.

The drink is closely related to the older Cuban Canchanchara. US Admiral Lucius Johnson tasted it with Cox in 1909 and brought it to the Army & Navy Club in Washington. Its first appearance in a recipe book was Jacques Straub's 1914 Drinks.
